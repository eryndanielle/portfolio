I'm Eryn Danielle, a New York based director of multitudes (art, creative, and movement) with a background in curatorial work, creative strategy, and dance. For 12 years I've shaped how brands show up in the world: from leading an award-winning print publication to helping beauty startups grow across borders, guiding a legacy publisher to find a new voice, and helping a secondhand fashion brand come to life right in the heart of Brooklyn.

My directorial lens is through movement. Trained as a dancer and movement artist, I direct fashion, film, and live performance the way choreography is directed: frame, lighting, gesture, and garment as part of one cohesive perspective. It's a point of view most art directors don't have, and it shows up in everything from editorial covers to gallery openings.

Secondhand is not a category I market. It's how I dress, shoot, source, and publish every day. Behind the work sits a creative network built over 12 years comprised of photographers, animators, videographers, DJs, lighting technicians, dancers, and artists I cast and place on real productions and projects.

Case Studies & Methodology

My directorial approach is rooted first in the concept and the research, then supported by the written brief, the reference set, and the visual direction. Then I hire and lead people who are brilliant at technical execution: designers, developers, printers, dancers, garment makers, videographers. CASE 01Editorial & Fashion

Editorial & Styling

Editorials I produced and developed end to end: concept, art direction, styling, movement direction, and in listed cases, photography. Built entirely on secondhand, vintage, and reworked fashion, collaborating with independent artists.

My role(s)Concept, Art Direction, Styling, Movement Direction, Photography* StakeholdersPersonal Portfolio and Collaborations CollaboratorsDancers, Photographers, Vintage Archivists, Independent Designers, Backdrop Artists
Concept

Each shoot starts from a single reference thesis: an era, a silhouette, a piece of music, a building, or a film reference. Secondhand garments are sourced and treated as living material essential to the narrative and physical expression.

Craft

Styling vintage and reworked pieces, then directing how the clothes move on the body. Trained as a movement artist, I direct fashion the way choreography is directed: framing, lighting, and gesture as integral decisions. Working with photographers and cinematographers to capture dynamic movement.

Execution

A multiyear portfolio of movement-based editorial work: a deep understanding of how to capture moving image that translates across platforms and resonates with audiences, and a community of artists and technicians who create best-in-class work.

CASE 02Nonprofit & Civic

Wartime

An award-winning cultural publication, directed from blank page to 50,000 copies.

My roleArt Director, Creative Producer StakeholdersExecutive Leadership CollaboratorsIllustrators, writers, designers, printers
Concept

I audited 50+ best-in-class print publications (magazine, newspaper) to define the standard, then conceived the format evolution: from a black and white pamphlet, to a full color magazine on heavy cardstock, to a tabloid-style Special Edition built for an unconventional distribution model through gyms, barbershops, bookstores, and community centers.

Craft

Led cover direction and interior spread systems where type and color do the storytelling. Directed illustrators and designers spread by spread, holding every page to the standard the audit set. Tested 10 versions of paper and cardstock to compare color analysis and physical feel before going to print.

Execution

Issue No. 3 won the 2021 Florida Print Award for Best Use of Color in Design. The tabloid model unlocked quarterly releases and 50,000 copies distributed nationwide.

CASE 05Commercial Art

Lyle Gallery, Sculpture in Motion

A live movement performance pitched, cast, and directed inside an artist's exhibition opening.

My roleConcept, Pitch, Casting, Movement Direction StakeholdersGallery, Exhibiting Artist CollaboratorsAlvin Ailey Professional Dancer
Concept

I pitched Lyle Gallery a live movement performance concept meant to engage visual artists, dancers, and audiences in a nontraditional performance experience that shifted how patrons and viewers engage with performance and art in commercial spaces. Blending the barriers between the dance and art worlds and dissolving hierarchies in both.

Craft

Casting the right dancer for sculptural paper garments, made by Karina Sharif, that are known to constrain movement. Then directing choreography in close collaboration with the gallery and artist so the performance honored the work rather than upstaging it. Featuring a renowned professional dancer from Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater wearing the sculptural work: Karina Sharif's wearable sculptures become the garment; the garment becomes choreography.

Execution

Inaugural performance delivered at the exhibition opening, featuring a sold out crowd of 100 guests. Collaborated with a videographer and photographer to capture the performance during a private and public showing, archiving the work permanently.

CASE 07Beauty & Wellness

Glossier & The Consistency Project

Retail as brand theater: pop-ups on two continents, and a zero-waste store you could stand inside.

My roleProduct & Brand Education StakeholdersFounders, C Suite CollaboratorsRetail Teams, Store Designers
Concept

A brand's physical space is its loudest and biggest footprint. At Glossier, the pop-up was the product story told in person to new audiences who had limited access to physically try and test the products. Glossier was strictly online or in New York only, so the introduction of the brand to new markets was crucial. Play, experimentation, and individuality were central in the physical buildout and customer experience.

Craft

I led product education and floor management through pop-ups in Toronto and London, turning training into the brand experience itself. Maintained brand ethos, product language, and the culture of customer greeting and exit to match the tone established in the New York City flagship.

Execution

Engaged deeply with regional staff and customers, both new to Glossier's product suite. 7 day pop ups drove strategy to quickly establish trust with the consumer base and build brand affinity in preparation for key e-commerce expansion into London and Toronto two months later.

MOMENT 08Publishing

Multicultural Marketing at PRH

Audience research that changed how a legacy publisher saw its readers, and the creative brief that proved the method.

RoleCreative Strategist OutcomeIndustry-leading Multicultural Marketing Team formed
Concept

I spearheaded the inaugural Multicultural Marketing Workshops, convening 30-plus executives, title marketers, and department leads, and led the audience research that defined reader personas across adult and children's imprints.

Craft

The workshops catalyzed an industry-leading Multicultural Marketing Team, with imprint-specific content strategy and dedicated media plans. And the method scaled down to a single title: my Such a Fun Age relaunch strategy, built from primary and secondary personas with psychographics down to brand affinities, comp-title and cover-art analysis, and messaging that embraced critical reviews instead of hiding from them.

Execution

The brief closes with written art direction for the design team: guidance on the racial and gendered connotations of typefaces, color beyond the gender binary, and imagery that centers the primary audience. Research became the brief, which then became direction for designers to be guided by.
